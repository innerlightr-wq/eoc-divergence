    The 3x + 1 map T is defined on the 2-adic integers Z2 by T (x) = x/2 for even x and
T (x) = (3x + 1)/2 for odd x.4 The trajectory T (x) is the infinite sequence

                          T (x) := (T 0 (x) = x, T 1 (x), T 2 (x), . . . , T k (x), . . .)

where T k (x) denotes the k’th iterate of T . The set

                                         O(x) := {T k (x) : k ∈ N0 }

is called the orbit of x. The orbit is finite if and only if the trajectory is eventually cyclic:
T i (x) = T j (x) for some i 6= j. If T k (x) = x for some k ≥ 1, then the trajectory is purely
cyclic, and O(x) contains the elements of a finite cycle. If O(x) is an infinite set, the
trajectory of the 2-adic integer x is called divergent.

    Let Qodd denote the ring of rational numbers having odd denominators in reduced fraction
form. This ring is isomorphic to the subring Q2 ⊂ Z2 of eventually periodic 2-adic integers.
Thus Qodd is the ring of rational 2-adic integers. The question ab to whether there is a
rational 2-adic integer with a divergent trajectory is part of the periodicity conjecture raised
in 1985 by Jeffrey C. Lagarias ([2]). Following ([2]), the trajectory of x evaluated modulo 2
is called the parity vector of x:
